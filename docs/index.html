Do home ice, road form or scoring style decide NHL games?

I tested five full seasons (2021-22 to 2025-26, 6,533 games) using only what each team had done before each game, the same information you have when you place a bet. Games before both teams had played 15 are left out, leaving 5,297 for the matchup tests.

53.7%
Home ice is real but small. Home teams win 53.7% of games (52.1% to 56.3% by season) and score 3.23 goals to the visitors' 3.01. In OT and shootouts the edge shrinks to 52.4%.
0.04
"Good at home" and "road warrior" records don't last. Across 160 team-seasons, how much better a team did at home than on the road in the first half had almost no link (correlation 0.04, where 1 is perfect and 0 is none) to the second half. A team's home and road records only track its overall quality.
GD
Goal differential is the best guide. Season goals-for minus goals-against per game picked the winner as well as the standings, with slightly better-calibrated odds. Once you know it, adding points %, home/road splits, last-10 form or scoring style didn't measurably improve predictions.
≈50/50
Scoring style doesn't decide winners. When two teams have similar goal differentials, the high-scoring "wild" team (lots of goals both ways) beat the low-scoring "tight" team 52% of the time, which is a coin flip (288 games). Whether a style clash helps the home team flipped direction from season to season.

About 580 games per cell, so each figure is accurate to roughly ±4 points.

  • High-scoring teams beat low-scoring teams 68% of the time at home and 61% on the road. Home ice is worth about 3–4 points either way. That is mostly because high-scoring teams are usually better teams. Compare teams with the same goal differential and the gap disappears.
  • Defence-first vs offence-first at the same goal differential: the offence-first team won 54% (112 games, ±9). That's not enough to bet on.
  • Totals barely move. Every cell averages 6.1–6.4 goals. Even when two high-scoring teams meet, the game averages only 6.38 goals against a league average of 6.24.

Over/under: who sets the pace?

  • Game totals move only about three-quarters as much as the two teams' scoring averages suggest. Extreme teams drift back toward league average.
  • When a tight team meets a wild team (1,198 games), the tight team does not slow the game down: totals averaged 6.31 and went over 6.5 46.5% of the time, the same as the league (46.1%). If anything, the higher-scoring team pulls the total up slightly more.

Standings vs goal differential

When the standings and goal differential disagree about which team is better, the team with the better goal differential won 56% (61% when it was at home). That supports the "standings can flatter a team" idea, but there were only 55 such games, so treat it as a lean, not proof.

What changed in the app because of this

  • Model weights: full-season goals 80% (was 45%), home/road split 10% (was 35%), last 10 10% (was 20%). League-wide home ice stays built in.
  • New reasons: Goal-differential gap and Standings flatter the other side.
  • Home/road records, road warriors, last-10 form and the season series are still shown in each game's breakdown but marked weak signal, and they no longer back an angle.

How to use this at Bet365

  1. Judge teams by goal differential per game, not the standings or their home/road record, and before about game 55 by expected-goal differential (round 2 below). The Teams tab shows both.
  2. Be wary of "they're great on the road" stories. They don't persist. If a price seems to lean on one, the other side may be the value. That's a hypothesis, not tested, because there's no free historical odds data.
  3. Be choosy with totals. Team styles barely move them, so lines near 6.5 are usually close to fair. Look for a goalie change or a back-to-back instead.

Round 2: what professional bettors use

Same method, five seasons, adding expected goals (shot quality, from MoneyPuck), luck, last season's ratings, schedule spots and the actual starting goalie for 12,939 team-games.

59% vs 55%
Shot quality beats goals until about game 55. Between games 15 and 30, expected goals picked the winner 59% of the time; goal differential and the standings managed 55%. By the last third of the season, actual goals catch up and pass it (62% vs 60%).
Hot ≠ good
Luck evens out. Teams whose even-strength shooting % + save % (PDO) is well above 1.000 are overrated by their record and goal differential, and cold teams are underrated. This was a reliable effect.
~8 games
Last season still matters in November. Between games 11 and 20, last season's goal differential predicted winners better than this season's. With shot quality in the mix it's still worth about 8 games of weight.
−7 pts
Back-to-backs are the only schedule spot that holds up. A road team on the second night loses about 8 points of win chance, and a home team about 6.5, even after accounting for the backup goalie. 3-in-4 nights, long road trips, the first game home and crossing time zones showed nothing reliable, and adding them made predictions worse on an unseen season.
3–4 pts
The starting goalie matters, but less than line moves suggest. A typical starter-vs-backup gap (about .010 in adjusted save %) is worth roughly 0.3 goals, or 3–4 points of win chance, and about half a goal off the total. Home teams starting a backup won 51.3% vs the 53.1% expected. Books often move 20–40 cents (5–9 points) on goalie news. Whether that over-moves is worth watching, but it's untested.
54.7%
Net result on a season the model never saw (2025-26): the new version picked 54.7% of winners vs 53.4% for the round-1 version, with better-calibrated odds. Small but real gains are what to expect: the books know most of this too.

What changed in the app (round 2)

  • Shot quality: each team's full-season rating is now all expected goals early in the season, shifting to 70% actual goals by the last third.
  • Last season: counts for about 8 games' worth early on, after being pulled a third of the way back to average.
  • Starting goalie picker on every game: projected from recent starts, switching to the other goalie when the usual starter played the night before. Change it once starters are confirmed and every number updates.
  • Back-to-back: effect raised from about 5 to about 7 points.
  • New reasons: Shot-quality edge, Running hot / Running cold, Goalie matchup.
  • Bet log: now tracks closing line value (see the Bet log tab).

What this can't tell you: whether these patterns beat the bookmakers' prices. That needs historical odds, which aren't freely available. The tests show what predicts winners, and the books know most of it too. What's closing line value?

Closing line value (CLV) is how pros grade themselves: did you get a better price than the market's final pre-game price? Beating the close consistently is the best sign of a real edge, and it shows up in 50 bets where wins and losses need hundreds. Moneylines logged from the slate fill in automatically from the books' consensus; for anything else, type the closing price you see at Bet365 just before puck drop.

How the numbers are built

Data refreshes automatically from the NHL's official stats feed several times a day. Nothing here needs an account or a key.

  1. Team ratings. Each team gets an attack and a defence rating against league average, blended from its full season (80%), home or road split (10%) and last 10 games (10%). The full-season part mixes expected goals (shot quality, from MoneyPuck) with actual goals, all expected goals early, 70% actual goals late, and leans on last season for the first couple of months. All weights come from the studies on the Research tab. Small samples are pulled toward league average so a three-game hot streak doesn't take over.
  2. Expected goals. League scoring at that venue × attack × the opponent's defence. Teams on the second night of a back-to-back lose about 7 points of win chance. The starting goalie scales the goals the other side is expected to score, based on his save % (this season plus last, pulled toward average) against the goaltending the team has had so far.
  3. Score grid. Those expected goals feed a score-probability grid, tuned to this season's real tie rate (games to OT) and empty-net goals. That produces win %, puck line %, totals and game props.
  4. Market check. Moneylines from several books (FanDuel, DraftKings and others in the NHL feed) are averaged with the bookmaker margin removed. When the model is 3+ points higher than the market and the stats give a reason, it's flagged as an angle.
  5. Reasons. Each game is scanned for mismatches: goal-differential gaps, standings that flatter a team, rest, power play vs penalty kill, goaltending, and expected goals. Home/road records, last-10 form and the season series are still listed in the breakdown but marked weak signal, because history shows they don't predict results, so they never back an angle.

Limits worth knowing

  • Starting goalies are projected, not confirmed: the NHL feed doesn't name starters in advance. The projection takes whoever started most of the last 10 games, or the other goalie on the second night of a back-to-back. Across 11,035 past team-games that was right about two-thirds of the time, better than any other rule tested (the last game's starter was right only 55%). Each projection is labelled Likely (70%+), Lean (about 60%) or Toss-up (about 50%), and leans and toss-ups show what the game looks like with the other goalie. Bets that only hold up with the projected goalie are flagged and kept out of the automatic parlay. Check the link on each game and change the picker once starters are confirmed.
  • Injuries and line changes aren't modelled.
  • Early in the season (under ~15 games) ratings are noisy and lean on league average.
  • Player props aren't covered, by design. Game props shown: overtime yes/no and team totals.
