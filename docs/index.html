Do home ice, road form or scoring style decide NHL games?

I tested five full seasons (2021-22 to 2025-26, 6,533 games) using only what each team had done before each game, the same information you have when you place a bet. Games before both teams had played 15 are left out, leaving 5,297 for the matchup tests.

53.7%
Home ice is real but small. Home teams win 53.7% of games (52.1% to 56.3% by season) and score 3.23 goals to the visitors' 3.01. In OT and shootouts the edge shrinks to 52.4%.
0.04
"Good at home" and "road warrior" records don't last. Across 160 team-seasons, how much better a team did at home than on the road in the first half had almost no link (correlation 0.04, where 1 is perfect and 0 is none) to the second half. A team's home and road records only track its overall quality.
GD
Goal differential is the best guide. Season goals-for minus goals-against per game picked the winner as well as the standings, with slightly better-calibrated odds. Once you know it, adding points %, home/road splits, last-10 form or scoring style didn't measurably improve predictions.
≈50/50
Scoring style doesn't decide winners. When two teams have similar goal differentials, the high-scoring "wild" team (lots of goals both ways) beat the low-scoring "tight" team 52% of the time, which is a coin flip (288 games). Whether a style clash helps the home team flipped direction from season to season.

About 580 games per cell, so each figure is accurate to roughly ±4 points.

  • High-scoring teams beat low-scoring teams 68% of the time at home and 61% on the road. Home ice is worth about 3–4 points either way. That is mostly because high-scoring teams are usually better teams. Compare teams with the same goal differential and the gap disappears.
  • Defence-first vs offence-first at the same goal differential: the offence-first team won 54% (112 games, ±9). That's not enough to bet on.
  • Totals barely move. Every cell averages 6.1–6.4 goals. Even when two high-scoring teams meet, the game averages only 6.38 goals against a league average of 6.24.

Over/under: who sets the pace?

  • Game totals move only about three-quarters as much as the two teams' scoring averages suggest. Extreme teams drift back toward league average.
  • When a tight team meets a wild team (1,198 games), the tight team does not slow the game down: totals averaged 6.31 and went over 6.5 46.5% of the time, the same as the league (46.1%). If anything, the higher-scoring team pulls the total up slightly more.

Standings vs goal differential

When the standings and goal differential disagree about which team is better, the team with the better goal differential won 56% (61% when it was at home). That supports the "standings can flatter a team" idea, but there were only 55 such games, so treat it as a lean, not proof.

What changed in the app because of this

  • Model weights: full-season goals 80% (was 45%), home/road split 10% (was 35%), last 10 10% (was 20%). League-wide home ice stays built in.
  • New reasons: Goal-differential gap and Standings flatter the other side.
  • Home/road records, road warriors, last-10 form and the season series are still shown in each game's breakdown but marked weak signal, and they no longer back an angle.

How the numbers are built

Data refreshes automatically from the NHL's official stats feed several times a day. Nothing here needs an account or a key.

  1. Team ratings. Each team gets an attack and a defence rating against league average, blended from its full season goals for and against (80%), home or road split (10%) and last 10 games (10%). Those weights come from the 5-season study on the Research tab. Small samples are pulled toward league average so a three-game hot streak doesn't take over.
  2. Expected goals. League scoring at that venue × attack × the opponent's defence. Teams on the second night of a back-to-back lose about 5%.
  3. Score grid. Those expected goals feed a score-probability grid, tuned to this season's real tie rate (games to OT) and empty-net goals. That produces win %, puck line %, totals and game props.
  4. Market check. Moneylines from several books (FanDuel, DraftKings and others in the NHL feed) are averaged with the bookmaker margin removed. When the model is 3+ points higher than the market and the stats give a reason, it's flagged as an angle.
  5. Reasons. Each game is scanned for mismatches: goal-differential gaps, standings that flatter a team, rest, power play vs penalty kill, goaltending, and expected goals. Home/road records, last-10 form and the season series are still listed in the breakdown but marked weak signal, because history shows they don't predict results, so they never back an angle.

Limits worth knowing

  • Starting goalies aren't confirmed in the feed until close to puck drop. Check the starter before betting; a backup in net can flip a lean.
  • Injuries and line changes aren't modelled.
  • Early in the season (under ~15 games) ratings are noisy and lean on league average.
  • Player props aren't covered, by design. Game props shown: overtime yes/no and team totals.
